Every great decision in history began as an argument someone was willing to make out loud. This is the story of the club built to teach that courage.

01
Our Story

Born from a single question no one could quite answer.

The Debate & Dialogue Club began not in a lecture hall, but in a corridor argument that refused to end. A handful of students, unwilling to let a good disagreement go to waste, decided to give it a proper stage. What started as informal Friday discussions grew into AIKTC's home for structured debate, Model UN diplomacy, and public discourse.

Today, the club carries that same restless curiosity — a conviction that every opinion deserves to be tested, and every student deserves the platform to test it.

02
Our Vision

A campus where every voice has been trained to matter.

"To cultivate confident communicators, critical thinkers, and future leaders through meaningful dialogue and intellectual excellence."

We measure our success not in trophies alone, but in the quiet moment a first-year finally raises their hand — certain, for the first time, that their argument holds.

03
Our Mission

Skills built on the floor, not just in the syllabus.

To provide an inclusive platform where students develop communication, leadership, analytical thinking, and public speaking skills through debates, discussions, workshops, and collaborative events.

Every session is designed with one outcome in mind: members who leave more articulate, more curious, and more prepared to lead than when they walked in.

Leadership Philosophy
“

We lead by handing the floor away, not holding onto it.

Our senior members are coaches first, captains second. Leadership at the Debate & Dialogue Club means creating the conditions for someone else's best argument — not delivering your own.

→

Mentorship over hierarchy

Every senior member is paired with newcomers for one-on-one coaching, not command.

→

Feedback as a gift

Every round ends in structured, specific, kind critique — never a verdict without a reason.

→

Earned responsibility

Committee roles rotate by contribution and consistency, never by seniority alone.
